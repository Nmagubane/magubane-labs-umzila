# Umzila: how it earns, and how it stays trusted

Labels: **FACT** (checked), **ASSUMPTION** (to test in the pilot), **RECOMMENDATION** (my call as your mentor).

## The rule that protects everything
**RECOMMENDATION:** Learners never pay, there are no adverts, learner data is never sold or shared with bursary providers, universities or sponsors, and no institution can pay to rank higher. A career tool lives or dies on trust. Parents and principals will forgive a missing feature, but not a hint that advice is for sale. This is also the only position that is safe with minors' data under POPIA.

## Who pays
1. **Schools (the main customer).** The buyer is the person who carries the subject-choice problem: the Life Orientation teacher, the Grade 9 head or a deputy principal. They pay for the class tools: the class report, mark-gap tracking and printable plans for parents' evenings.
   - **ASSUMPTION:** fee-paying public schools and independent schools will pay a per-learner annual licence for Grades 9–12. Starting hypothesis to test: **R30–R60 per learner per year**. Do not publish a price until five pilot schools have reacted to it.
   - **FACT:** no-fee schools are quintiles 1–3. They will not pay, and they are where the Maths vs Maths Lit problem hurts most. They are served through sponsors.
2. **Sponsors** (mining houses, banks, engineering firms, SETAs, foundations) pay for licences for no-fee schools, usually in the communities where they operate.
   - **ASSUMPTION:** sponsorship may count towards companies' social and labour plan or B-BBEE socio-economic development commitments. Check this with a B-BBEE adviser before you pitch it as such.
   - Sponsors get aggregate impact reporting (for example, "learners who switched to Mathematics after a class conversation"), never named learner data.
3. **Later: provincial departments and districts**, once pilot evidence exists.

## Why a school would pay (the evidence to collect in the pilot)
- How many learners would have closed their own goal before subject choices were final, and how many changed after a conversation (the class report counts this).
- Teacher time saved at subject-choice season.
- Parent attendance and feedback at subject-choice evenings that used printed plans.

## How it fits with Bonisa
Umzila tells a learner *what mark they need*; Bonisa helps them *get it*. Sell them together to schools, and add "practise this on Bonisa" next to each mark target once the products share a sign-in.

## The real cost
**FACT:** university requirements change every year. Keeping the data right is the main ongoing cost, not hosting. Budget about two weeks each February–April for the prospectus update (docs/DATA.md). Coverage should grow with revenue: more careers and institutions are the clearest thing schools are paying for.

## Pilot plan (RECOMMENDATION)
1. Verify the flagged data (docs/DATA.md) and confirm the product name.
2. Run 3–5 schools in Term 1 2027, timed for Grade 9 subject choices in Terms 2–3. Include at least one no-fee school with a sponsor.
3. Measure: share of learners with a goal, closed routes caught and resolved, weekly teacher use, and what schools say they would pay.
4. Set the price, and add billing only then.
